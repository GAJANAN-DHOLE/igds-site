### 1. Start with the motor

Note the rating in HP, the rated current on the nameplate and the supply voltage. A starter has a range it is built for, such as 2 to 7.5 HP on 415 VAC. Choose the one that covers your motor without running at its limit.

### 2. Choose the starting method

- **Direct on line** connects the motor straight to the supply. It is the simplest and the usual choice for small pumps.
- **Star-delta** reduces the starting current for larger motors.
- **Soft starters** ramp the voltage for a gentler start.

### 3. Assess the supply

Rural supply swings, drops a phase or arrives in the wrong sequence. If your site does this, you need the starter to measure voltage and act on it, and adjustable low and high limits.

### 4. Specify the protections

For a pump, the ones that matter most are dry run and under load, overload, phase fail and sequence, voltage unbalance, and under and over voltage. Look for the ones you cannot see from the road: stator and cable faults.

### 5. Decide how it will be operated

Standing at the panel is one option. A GSM link lets you start and stop from a phone, and a timer or cycle controller removes the visit altogether.

### A short checklist

Motor HP and current, supply voltage, the faults your site sees, and how you want to switch it on.
