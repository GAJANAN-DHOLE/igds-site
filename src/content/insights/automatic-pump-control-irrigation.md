### What manual operation costs

Someone has to be at the field to switch the pump on and, more importantly, off. Pumps run too long, run dry, or wait for a trip that only happens when the supply returns. Each of those costs water, power or a motor.

### Three levels of automation

1. **Remote operation.** A GSM link starts and stops the pump from a phone, and reports faults. It saves the journey.
2. **Scheduled operation.** A timer or a run-and-rest cycle waters on a fixed pattern, with no one calling.
3. **Condition-based control.** Level sensors or a water-level controller start and stop the pump from what the tank or well actually holds.

### What improves

- **Water.** Pumping stops when the job is done.
- **Energy.** Less running time and fewer starts on the wrong supply.
- **Motor and pump life.** Dry run, phase failure and unbalance are caught before they damage anything.
- **Labour.** Fewer trips to the field.

### Choosing a level

Begin with remote operation if the main problem is distance. Add a cycle timer if watering is predictable, and add level control where a tank or well is part of the system. The starter should carry the protections underneath all three.
