### How each acquires the image

Analog mammography exposes a film inside a screen-film cassette, which is then chemically processed. Digital mammography uses a flat-panel detector that converts the exposure to an electronic image straight away.

### What changes

- **Dynamic range.** Film responds well inside a narrow band of exposure, and dense tissue and thin edges compete for it. A digital detector records a wider range, and display settings can be adjusted afterwards without another exposure.
- **Repeats.** A poorly exposed film is a repeat. A digital image can often be adjusted on screen first.
- **Workflow.** No film processing, no film storage. Images go to the archive over DICOM and can be viewed on a workstation.
- **Tomosynthesis.** Layered imaging of the breast needs a digital detector. Film cannot offer it.

### Upgrading an analog system

A full replacement is not the only route. If the mechanical gantry is sound, a retrofit can replace the film cassette with a digital detector and add control software, calibration and DICOM output. What needs care is the exposure interface, detector calibration and the new quality-control routine.

### Where that leaves analog

Analog units remain in service. The question for each is whether keeping film is still worth what it limits.
