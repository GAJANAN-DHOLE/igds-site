### The hazard

An MRI magnet is switched on all the time. A ferromagnetic object brought near it is pulled towards the bore, so a stray wheelchair, tool or gas cylinder becomes a projectile. Sites manage this with access zones: Zone IV is the scanner room itself, and Zone III is the controlled area outside it.

### Where detection fits

A ferromagnetic detector stands at the boundary between those zones. It is an additional check beside the screening questionnaire, and it does not replace it.

### How it differs from a metal detector

A conventional metal detector sends out a field and reacts to anything conductive, including non-magnetic metals that are not a risk. A passive detector emits nothing. It uses magnetometers to sense the disturbance that a moving ferromagnetic object makes in the surrounding magnetic field, so it responds to the objects that matter.

### Engineering considerations

- Rejecting noise from lifts, vehicles and mains wiring nearby
- An adjustable detection threshold
- A vertical sensor array, so the height of the object is known
- Electronics that cope with the fringe field at the site
- Visual and audible alarm, with a door interlock where the site wants one

### Limits worth stating

It detects ferromagnetic objects only, and generally those that move through the detection zone. Implants, small items and objects carried slowly may not be caught, so screening procedures stay in place.
